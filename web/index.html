Method and limits

What it reads, and what it cannot see.

A LeRobot teleoperation recording stores the leader arm (the command, action) and the follower arm (observation.state) at every frame. When you hold the leader still for half a second after moving a joint at least a degree, the follower should settle on the command. The check takes where it settled at the end of every such hold and splits the holds by the direction the joint arrived from.

Lost motion is the width of a play band fitted to those settled errors, replaying the command from where the approach began; the 90% interval comes from resampling the holds. Scatter is the robust spread of the settled error from one side. A joint needs at least 8 holds from above and 8 from below. The step size is read from the recording itself, so degrees and the -100..100 range both work.

At the gripper, each joint's scatter and half its lost motion go through the SO-101 arm's geometry at 64 table poses 13 to 25 cm in front of the base, tool pointing down. The figure is the sideways distance that holds 95% of arrivals. SO-100 links differ slightly; the same geometry is used.

The comparison is 371 SO-100 and SO-101 arms measured the same way from public LeRobot recordings, one value per arm; 280 of them have all five joints measured for the gripper figures.

LimitThe servo's encoder sits on its output shaft, so play between the shaft and the link (horn screws, printed parts, link bending) does not appear. Every figure here is a lower bound on the play at the tool.
LimitA hold where the arm presses on the table or an object reads as error; medians limit this, they do not remove it. Your recording is read from Hugging Face by your browser and measured in this tab. Private and gated datasets cannot be read here.
